SCRIBECUE

Scribecue needs a computer

It's an editor for audio description scripts, built for a keyboard and a big screen: you hold keys while the narrator speaks, step through the video frame by frame, and work on a timeline. That isn't possible on a phone or tablet.

Open scribecue.kyleadvoice.ca on a computer, in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge.

      Welcome to Scribecue
      
      
      Scribecue is a keyboard-first editor for audio description scripts. Your video and your
        script stay on your computer: nothing is uploaded.
      
      
      This quick setup picks the tools you need. You can change any of it later, and run it
        again from Settings at the top.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      What are you working on?
      
      
      
      
      You can switch at any time with the Transcribe / Write switch at the top.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Hold-to-mark
      
      
      Play the video, hold H while the narrator speaks a line, and let go when they stop.
        A block appears, ready for you to type the line.
      
      
      
      
      Both edges of a mark move back by this much, because your key press comes a moment
        after you hear the voice. 200 ms suits most people.
      
      
      
      When you press Esc or ↵, the playhead goes back to the block's
        start, so it plays again, then carries on. ⇧L turns it on or off any time.

        Natural voices Recommended
        
        
        Piper voices sound more natural, give exact lengths straight away, and let you export
          a description track. They run in your browser, so your script stays on your computer. Each voice is
          a one-time download, and then it works offline too.
        
        
        
          
          
          
        
        
        
        
        Want voices that sound even more like a person reading? Kokoro voices
          are free too, but need Scribecue running on your computer with Docker: they're too big and slow to
          run well in a browser.
          How to add Kokoro
        
      
        
      
      
      
        
        
        
          
           dB
        
        
        
      
        
      
      
        Each block ends where its reading ends. To make a block longer or shorter, change its speed.
        Blocks opened from a file keep their timing until you edit them.
        
      
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        You're using a Google Cloud voice, so making all readings, updating all timings or fitting speeds to timings reads every block with Google, apart from lines it has already read at the same voice and speed. That uses up your free characters, and may cost money once they run out.
        
      
        Make all readings
        
      
        Read every block now, at its own speed, so each one plays straight away and its
        length is exact. Timings aren't changed. The readings are kept in this browser, so they're still
        ready next time, and work offline.
        
      
        
        
         
        
      
        
      
        Update all timings
        
      
        Set the end of every block from how long its reading takes, with each block's own
        speed. This includes blocks marked with H and blocks opened from a file. Ends never run into the
        next block, and Z undoes it.
        
      
      
        
        
         
        
      
        Fit speeds to timings
        
      
        The other way round: every block keeps its timing, and its reading speeds up or slows
        down to fill it, between −15% and +100%. Useful for a script opened from a file. Blocks with a speed
        you picked keep it, and Z undoes it.
        
      
        
        
         
        
      
        Export description track
        
      
        Save every block's reading as one audio file, each starting at its block's start
        time, ready to mix with the programme. Readings peak at −8 dBFS, leaving headroom for
        processing and mastering. An MP3 is much smaller than a WAV, and fine for sharing or
        checking; for the final mix, a WAV keeps everything.
        
      
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        Export full mix
        
      
        Save the programme's sound and the readings together in one stereo file, as you
        hear them when you play: the programme is lowered under each reading as set above, or as the block
        says, and the levels follow the mix slider under the video. It uses the audio track that's
        playing. A loudness target brings the whole mix to that level; either way, peaks stay under
        −1 dBFS (−2 for ATSC).
        
      
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        With ffmpeg: in the video, the mix is AAC, or AC-3 or E-AC-3 if chosen
        above (Opus in a WebM). The picture and other tracks are copied as they are. The video is sent to
        ffmpeg on this computer, so Docker needs room for a copy while it works.

        Google Cloud voices
        
      
        Google's Standard voices, with your own Google Cloud key. Like Piper voices, they give
        exact lengths and can be exported as a description track. Each reading sends the block's text to
        Google, and Google bills your Google Cloud account for it: Standard voices have a free allowance
        each month. See Google's prices.
        
      
        
        
        Get a key
        
        
      
        
      
        The key needs the Cloud Text-to-Speech API turned on in its project. It's kept in this
        browser, and only sent to Google.
        The Google voices guide
        covers setting up the key, and making sure you're never billed. Readings are kept in this browser,
        so each line is only sent to Google once, unless you change it.
        
      
        Online voices
        
      
      
        This browser doesn't offer any online voices. To use
        them, open Scribecue in Microsoft Edge, which has the most, or Google Chrome. Safari and Firefox
        only have the voices on your computer.
        
      
        Online voices often sound more natural, but each block's text is sent to the
        company that runs the voice, such as Google or Microsoft, to be spoken. Leave this off if your
        script mustn't leave your computer. The voices built into your computer keep everything on it.

      Did you know? Recommended
      
        Scribecue can use Piper, a free, open-source speech engine that runs next to it in Docker.
        Its voices sound much more natural, stay on your computer, and work offline. With a Piper voice, reading
        lengths are exact straight away, and you can export a description track as a WAV or MP3 file.
        
      
        To add it, put COMPOSE_PROFILES=piper in a file called .env in the Scribecue folder, then
        run docker compose up -d --build and reload this page.
        More about Piper
        
      
        For even more lifelike voices, add kokoro too, as in COMPOSE_PROFILES=piper,kokoro.
        Kokoro is about 1.1 GB, and reads a little slower.

          Speech to text
          
    
          Whisper listens to each block you mark with H and types what the narrator says,
      ready for you to check. You still time every line yourself. It runs on your computer, so the
      audio never leaves it.
          
    
          
    
          
      
          Whisper is a one-time download into your browser, and then it works offline
        too. It's fastest in Chrome or Edge on a computer with a graphics chip they can use.
          
      
          
        
        
        
          
    
          
    
          
      
          
    
          
    
    
          When you let go of H, the block's text appears after a moment.
      If you start typing first, Whisper leaves the block to you.
          
    
          
      
      
    
          
    
          Transcribe empty blocks
          
    
          Fill in every block that has no text yet, such as blocks drawn on the timeline or
      marked with this off. Z undoes it.
          
    
          
      
       
          
    
          Whisper understands English. It hears everything in a block, so dialogue or
      lyrics under the narration can come out too: if your file has a description track on its own,
      choose it with T. Names are often misspelled, so check them.
      The transcribing guide
      covers all of it.
          
    
          
      
    
          
            

  
    
          
      
          Check for updates?
          
      
          Scribecue can check GitHub once a day and tell you when a new version is out.
          
      
          GitHub sees the request and your IP address, like any website visit. Nothing about
        your files or text blocks is sent. If you say no, Scribecue never contacts anything outside
        your computer.
          
      
          
        
        
      
          
      
          You can change this later in Settings, under General.
